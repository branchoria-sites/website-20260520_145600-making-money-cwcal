## Introduction

The Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) ruling against MailOnline became an important UK affiliate-marketing case because it showed that weak disclosure [wording]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-disclosure-9d7a11-clear-affiliate-word-ac25e8/' | relative_url }}) is not enough when an article looks like ordinary journalism but exists mainly to drive affiliate sales. The ruling mattered far beyond newspapers. It affected [affiliate review]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-review-pag-f66da7/' | relative_url }}) sites, “best products” blogs, shopping roundups, and magazine-style recommendation pages that rely on affiliate commission.


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_disclosure_9d7a11_mailonline_disclosur_295b02-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="MailOnline case illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_disclosure_9d7a11_mailonline_disclosur_295b02-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_disclosure_9d7a11_mailonline_disclosur_295b02-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
The key lesson was not simply “add a disclaimer”. MailOnline already had [disclosure]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-review-pag-f66da7-affiliate-disclosure-8b10cd/' | relative_url }}) text on several pages. The problem was that the wording and page design still made the content appear primarily editorial rather than commercial. The ASA decided that phrases such as “may earn an affiliate commission” and claims that products were “independently selected” did not clearly explain the commercial reality to readers. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/associated-newspapers-ltd-g22-1153503-associated-newspapers-ltd.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">associated newspapers ltd g22 1153503 associated newspapers ltd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAAssociated Newspapers Ltd21 Dec 2022 — If you make a purchase using links on this page, [we / MailOnline] may earn an affiliate commis...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/spring-2023/the-asas-strict-approach-to-affiliate-marketing-links-and-the-need-for-advertising-disclosures/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rpclegal.com">[RPC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rpclegal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RPCThe ASA&#x27;s strict approach to affiliate marketing links and...31 Mar 2023 — Further, the ASA deemed the statement “…[we / MailOnline]...</span></span></span>

For affiliate websites built to generate revenue at scale, the case is useful because it demonstrates how regulators assess the whole page experience: layout, wording, commercial intent, link structure, and whether the article would likely exist without the affiliate arrangement.

## Why the articles were treated as ads

The MailOnline ruling centred on shopping-style articles promoting products through affiliate links, including Amazon affiliate arrangements. MailOnline argued that the pages were editorial articles containing affiliate links rather than advertisements in their entirety. The ASA disagreed for several of the pages investigated. ASA <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/affiliate-links-make-for-bad-news/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com">[2marketinglaw]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">affiliate links make for bad news</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">links make for bad news25 Jan 2023 — A number of articles, featuring affiliate links, were published via MailOnline and www.dailymail.co...</span></span></span>

A major reason was the commercial structure behind the content. The ASA concluded that the articles were “wholly concerned with promoting the products” available through affiliate links. In practical terms, the regulator looked beyond the publisher’s branding and asked a more direct question:

Would these pages probably exist in the same form without the [affiliate revenue]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-conversion-fac650-affiliate-revenue-le-8c2b2f/' | relative_url }}) opportunity?

The ASA’s answer was effectively no. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAOnline Affiliate Marketing22 Mar 2023 — In one formal ruling, the ASA assessed a series of Instagram stories that featured the affilia...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/online-affiliate-marketing-new-cap-advice-note/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rpclegal.com">[RPC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rpclegal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">online affiliate marketing new cap advice note</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Online Affiliate Marketing: New CAP advice note2 Jun 2020 — Affiliate marketing must be obviously identifiable and must not mislead mater...</span></span></span>

That distinction matters for affiliate-site operators because many publishers still assume that editorial styling protects them from advertising rules. The ruling showed that editorial presentation does not override commercial intent. If the page’s real purpose is product promotion tied to commission-based links, the ASA may treat the whole page as advertising.

The regulator also examined how the products were selected. MailOnline stated that products were “independently selected by our shopping writers”. However, the ASA noted that some products were chosen from lists curated through affiliate arrangements, weakening the claim of purely independent editorial selection. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/associated-newspapers-ltd-g22-1153503-associated-newspapers-ltd.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">associated newspapers ltd g22 1153503 associated newspapers ltd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAAssociated Newspapers Ltd21 Dec 2022 — If you make a purchase using links on this page, [we / MailOnline] may earn an affiliate commis...</span></span></span>

For affiliate-content systems, this is a critical operational point. Many large-scale affiliate sites mix genuine editorial judgement with commercial filtering logic. Problems arise when pages imply complete editorial independence while monetisation partnerships strongly shape product selection.

The ASA also considered the wider visual environment of the pages. The articles resembled ordinary MailOnline journalism, including:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* editorial-style layouts
* related-article modules
* sharing widgets
* comment prompts
* standard newsroom formatting

</div>

These signals reinforced the impression that readers were consuming neutral journalism rather than commercial content. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAOnline Affiliate Marketing22 Mar 2023 — In one formal ruling, the ASA assessed a series of Instagram stories that featured the affilia...</span></span></span>

This matters for website monetisation systems because page templates affect legal interpretation. A highly commercial affiliate page disguised as mainstream editorial can create more disclosure risk than a clearly commercial “Deals”, “Shopping”, or “Best Buys” section.

## What made the disclaimer too weak

The most quoted part of the ruling concerned MailOnline’s wording:

<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/associated-newspapers-ltd-g22-1153503-associated-newspapers-ltd.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[“If you make a purchase using links on this page, we may earn an affiliate commission.”]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">associated newspapers ltd g22 1153503 associated newspapers ltd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAAssociated Newspapers Ltd21 Dec 2022 — If you make a purchase using links on this page, [we / MailOnline] may earn an affiliate commis...</span></span></span>

The ASA said this wording was ambiguous because, in normal circumstances, commission would in fact be earned when a qualifying purchase occurred. The phrase “may earn” implied uncertainty where the commercial arrangement was actually predictable and intentional. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAOnline Affiliate Marketing22 Mar 2023 — In one formal ruling, the ASA assessed a series of Instagram stories that featured the affilia...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/online-affiliate-marketing-new-cap-advice-note/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rpclegal.com">[RPC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rpclegal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">online affiliate marketing new cap advice note</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Online Affiliate Marketing: New CAP advice note2 Jun 2020 — Affiliate marketing must be obviously identifiable and must not mislead mater...</span></span></span>

This sounds like a small wording issue, but it exposed a wider regulatory concern: publishers often soften disclosure language to reduce friction and preserve editorial trust signals.

From a conversion perspective, softer wording may appear attractive because it feels less commercial. However, the ruling showed that ambiguity itself can become a compliance risk.

The ASA considered the revised wording — “MailOnline will earn an affiliate commission” — clearer because it described the commercial relationship directly. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAOnline Affiliate Marketing22 Mar 2023 — In one formal ruling, the ASA assessed a series of Instagram stories that featured the affilia...</span></span></span>

Another problem was that the disclosure focused narrowly on the links rather than the nature of the page as a whole. Readers were told commission could arise from purchases, but not clearly told that the article itself functioned as advertising content.

That distinction is important for affiliate publishers using editorial-style content templates. A disclosure can fail even when technically visible if it frames the affiliate relationship too narrowly. Regulators increasingly assess whether the average reader understands:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--benefit" markdown="1">

* why the article exists
* how product selection is influenced
* whether the publisher financially benefits from recommendations
* whether the page should reasonably be understood as commercial content

</div>

The ruling also showed that visibility alone is not enough. MailOnline’s disclosure text was not hidden entirely. The ASA acknowledged that readers probably would not overlook it. The issue was that the wording still failed to communicate the true commercial nature of the content. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAOnline Affiliate Marketing22 Mar 2023 — In one formal ruling, the ASA assessed a series of Instagram stories that featured the affilia...</span></span></span>

This is a useful warning for affiliate-site operators who rely on legalistic boilerplate. A disclosure can be technically present yet still inadequate if it leaves readers with the wrong overall impression.

## Why editorial styling increases disclosure risk

One of the most useful lessons from the MailOnline case is that disclosure requirements become stricter when affiliate content imitates journalism closely.

A plainly commercial page usually creates fewer recognition problems. For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* voucher directories
* cashback portals
* “today’s deals” pages
* obvious shopping hubs

</div>

Readers already expect monetisation on those [page types]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-buyer-intent-revenue-fe10e4-affiliate-page-types-956d68/' | relative_url }}).

The risk rises when the page adopts signals associated with independent editorial judgement, such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* product-review language
* magazine layouts
* expert recommendations
* newsroom branding
* lifestyle journalism tone
* “best products” rankings presented as neutral advice

</div>

The MailOnline ruling effectively confirmed that the more a page resembles independent editorial coverage, the clearer the commercial disclosure needs to be. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAOnline Affiliate Marketing22 Mar 2023 — In one formal ruling, the ASA assessed a series of Instagram stories that featured the affilia...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAOnline Affiliate Marketing22 Mar 2023 — In one formal ruling, the ASA assessed a series of Instagram stories that featured the affilia...</span></span></span>

This has direct implications for scalable affiliate publishing systems. A site generator that mass-produces editorial-style affiliate pages cannot treat disclosure as a tiny footer element added at the end of production. Disclosure logic needs to be connected to page classification.

For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* a coupon page may need lighter disclosure because commercial intent is obvious
* a product comparison article may require stronger top-of-page labelling
* a “best products” feature styled like journalism may need explicit “Ad” or “Advertising Feature” framing

</div>

The commercial ambiguity of the format changes the disclosure burden.

## How publishers can write clearer commission labels

The ruling did not ban affiliate content or require publishers to abandon editorial recommendation formats. Instead, it showed that disclosure wording should describe the relationship plainly and early.

Practical improvements include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* using direct language instead of hedged wording
* explaining commission before readers engage deeply with recommendations
* avoiding exaggerated claims of editorial independence
* making the commercial nature clear at article level where appropriate

</div>

Examples closer to current ASA expectations include:

* “This article contains affiliate links and we will earn commission if you buy through them.”
* “Advertisement feature: we earn commission from purchases made through links in this article.”
* “Shopping article containing affiliate links.”

The ASA and CAP guidance consistently favour clarity over euphemism. Terms such as “affiliate”, “partner link”, or abbreviated labels may not be sufficiently understood by ordinary readers on their own. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAOnline Affiliate Marketing22 Mar 2023 — In one formal ruling, the ASA assessed a series of Instagram stories that featured the affilia...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=44cd35e4-7387-4c2e-bd32-f5eead573eb4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lexology.com">[Lexology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lexology.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LexologyThe ASA&#x27;s strict approach to affiliate marketing links and...30 Mar 2023 — What does the ASA&#x27;s ruling on several MailOnline arti...</span></span></span>

## What the case changed for affiliate website strategy

The MailOnline ruling helped shift UK affiliate compliance discussions away from narrow link disclosure and towards whole-page commercial intent.

That has several practical consequences for monetised website systems.

### Page templates matter

A publisher cannot rely on editorial styling while treating disclosure as an afterthought. Templates influence regulatory interpretation. Pages that resemble journalism need stronger upfront commercial labelling.

### Product-selection claims need caution

Claims such as “independently selected”, “editor’s choice”, or “expert picks” become risky if affiliate partnerships heavily influence inclusion logic.

For scalable affiliate systems, it is safer to describe the recommendation process honestly rather than imply total editorial separation from monetisation incentives.

### Weak hedging language creates avoidable risk

Phrases such as:

* “may earn”
* “could receive”
* “partnered links”

often exist because publishers want softer commercial framing. The MailOnline ruling showed that ambiguity can itself become part of the compliance problem. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAOnline Affiliate Marketing22 Mar 2023 — In one formal ruling, the ASA assessed a series of Instagram stories that featured the affilia...</span></span></span>

### Entire-page classification matters

The ASA increasingly looks at whether the page as a whole functions commercially. This affects not only disclosure wording but also page architecture, section labelling, and internal-linking strategies.

For example, clearly separating editorial news coverage from shopping-driven affiliate hubs may reduce confusion for readers and regulators alike.

## Why the case still matters for affiliate publishers

The MailOnline ruling became influential because it involved a major mainstream publisher rather than a small niche affiliate site. That made the ASA’s position harder to dismiss as a rule aimed only at influencers or amateur bloggers.

The decision reinforced a broader UK trend: affiliate marketing is judged by how consumers actually experience the page, not merely by whether a disclosure technically exists somewhere on it. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAOnline Affiliate Marketing22 Mar 2023 — In one formal ruling, the ASA assessed a series of Instagram stories that featured the affilia...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/online-affiliate-marketing-new-cap-advice-note/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rpclegal.com">[RPC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rpclegal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">online affiliate marketing new cap advice note</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Online Affiliate Marketing: New CAP advice note2 Jun 2020 — Affiliate marketing must be obviously identifiable and must not mislead mater...</span></span></span>

For operators building revenue-focused affiliate websites, the practical lesson is straightforward. A disclosure strategy should not aim to preserve the illusion of neutrality while quietly collecting commission. It should aim to make the commercial relationship understandable without destroying usability or trust.

In practice, clearer disclosure often supports stronger long-term monetisation. Pages that honestly signal commercial intent are less exposed to regulatory complaints, reputational damage, and template-wide compliance failures that become expensive when scaled across thousands of affiliate URLs.
