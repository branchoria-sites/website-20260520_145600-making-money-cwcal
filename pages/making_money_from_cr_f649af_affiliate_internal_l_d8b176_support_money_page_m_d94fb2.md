## Introduction

A support article should not send every reader to the same affiliate page. It should send them to the next page that best matches what they are trying to do now.


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_internal_l_d8b176_support_money_page_m_d94fb2-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Page Mapping illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_internal_l_d8b176_support_money_page_m_d94fb2-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_internal_l_d8b176_support_money_page_m_d94fb2-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
That distinction matters because [affiliate revenue]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-conversion-fac650-affiliate-revenue-le-8c2b2f/' | relative_url }}) usually comes from movement through a sequence of decisions rather than from a single pageview. A reader searching “how much power does a robot vacuum need for pet hair?” is not yet in the same state as someone searching “best robot vacuums for Labrador hair”. The first reader needs narrowing and reassurance. The second is ready to compare products.

Support-to-money page mapping is the process of deciding which commercial page each informational article should lead towards. On a well-structured affiliate site, support pages guide readers into the right comparison, review, shortlist, or buyer page at the correct moment. On a weak site, informational pages either fail to guide readers anywhere useful or push readers into mismatched sales pages too early.

The quality of this mapping affects conversion rate, user trust, pages per visit, and revenue per visitor. It also affects how scalable a site becomes, because clear mapping rules make it easier to generate new content clusters systematically rather than manually deciding links page by page.

Google’s guidance on internal links and [anchor text]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-internal-l-d8b176-commercial-anchor-te-cda81b/' | relative_url }}) repeatedly stresses relevance, clarity, and helping users understand destination pages. Nielsen Norman Group research on “information scent” reaches a similar conclusion from a usability angle: users follow links that appear most likely to solve their current problem. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[Google for Developers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search CentralLink best practices for Google · Make your links crawlable...</span></span></span>

## Why the “highest commission” approach often loses money

Many affiliate sites make the same mistake: every support article points to the page with the highest payout product or merchant.

This often reduces total earnings rather than increasing them.

If a visitor lands on a guide about “how to choose hiking boot sizes”, sending them directly to a luxury £300 boot review may produce fewer clicks and lower trust than sending them to a broader “best hiking boots for wide feet” comparison page. The reader has not yet narrowed the problem enough to evaluate a premium product review.

The mismatch creates three problems:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The reader feels pushed instead of helped.
* The linked page has weak information scent because it does not obviously continue the user journey.
* The commercial page receives low-quality traffic with weak buying intent.

</div>

Internal links work best when they continue the reader’s decision path naturally. Google specifically recommends descriptive, relevant anchor text because it helps users and search engines understand what comes next. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[Google for Developers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search CentralLink best practices for Google · Make your links crawlable...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://yoast.com/internal-linking-for-seo-why-and-how/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yoast.com">[Yoast In practice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yoast.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YoastThe ultimate guide to internal linking for SEO and GEO12 Nov 2025 — When creating internal links...</span></span></span>, the best-converting destination is often:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* the most contextually relevant page, <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://medium.com/the-seo-channel/my-5-golden-rules-for-internal-linking-8dcc44bd49b6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medium.com">[medium.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

## How to classify the reader’s current intent

Good support-to-money mapping starts by identifying what the reader still needs before buying.

A practical affiliate content system can classify support articles into several intent stages.

### Problem-definition intent

These readers are still understanding the problem itself.

Examples:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* “What size air purifier do I need?”
* “Do standing desks help posture?”
* “Why does my lawn mower leave stripes?”

</div>

Readers here usually need:

* explanations,
* sizing logic,
* terminology,
* tradeoffs,
* or diagnostic help.

The best commercial destination is rarely a single-product review. More often it is:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* a filtered comparison page,
* a “best for” shortlist,
* or a category guide organised around the exact problem.

</div>

A page explaining dehumidifier extraction rates should usually link to:

* “Best dehumidifiers for damp bedrooms”

rather than:
* “MeacoDry ABC 12L review”.

### Category-selection intent

These readers understand the problem but not the solution category.

Examples:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* “Air fryer vs convection oven”
* “Mirrorless vs DSLR for beginners”
* “Corded vs cordless hedge trimmers”

</div>

These articles work well as bridges into:

* comparison hubs,
* category roundups,
* and segmented buying guides.

The reader is choosing a type of product, not yet a specific model.

### Feature-priority intent

These readers already know the category and are prioritising attributes.

Examples:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* “Best laptops for battery life”
* “Quiet coffee grinders”
* “Office chairs for short people”

</div>

This is often the strongest bridge into money pages because the buying [criteria]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-comparison-5755f1-category-specific-cr-a92f21/' | relative_url }}) are already explicit.

* preserve the exact feature focus,
* continue the same vocabulary,
* and reduce the number of choices.

A support article about mattress firmness for side sleepers should not jump to a general mattress homepage. It should link into a highly aligned buyer page such as:

* “Best mattresses for side sleepers under 80kg”.

### Near-purchase intent

These readers are already evaluating options.

Examples:

* “Ninja AF400 vs Cosori Dual Blaze”
* “Is the Kindle Paperwhite worth it?”
* “Best price for Garmin Forerunner 265”

Support pages at this stage can link directly to:

* individual reviews,
* retailer pages,
* deal pages,
* or tightly constrained comparisons.

The reader no longer needs broad educational content.

## How to choose the next commercial page

The simplest rule is:

The next page should reduce uncertainty without forcing a bigger decision than the reader is ready to make.

That sounds obvious, but it changes how affiliate sites structure clusters.

### Match the unresolved question

Each support article leaves the reader with a remaining question.

The destination should answer the unresolved commercial question, not restart the buying journey from scratch.

### Preserve topic continuity

Strong mappings maintain consistent language and framing.

If the support article repeatedly discusses:

* “small gardens”,
* “pet-safe cleaning”,
* or “budget beginner cameras”,

the linked commercial page should preserve those concepts.

This creates stronger information scent. Users are more likely to click links that clearly continue their goal path. ### Narrow before broadening

High-performing affiliate systems often move readers into progressively narrower commercial pages before exposing broader category hubs.

A reader researching “best coffee grinder for espresso” usually converts better on:

* a focused espresso grinder comparison,

than on:
* a general coffee equipment homepage.

Specificity increases commercial alignment.

This is one reason topic-cluster structures work well for affiliate sites. ## Examples of good and bad destination matches

### Good match: problem diagnosis to filtered comparison

## Signals that your support-to-money mapping is weak

Several patterns usually indicate poor mapping.

### High traffic, low commercial-page visits

This often means:

* support pages attract readers,
* but internal links fail to match intent.

The problem is frequently relevance rather than traffic quality.

### Strong click-through but weak affiliate clicks

Readers may click internal links but abandon the destination quickly.

Common causes:

* destination too broad,
* commercial pressure too early,
* or mismatch between promised and delivered content.

### Repeated links to the same money page

This often happens on affiliate sites built around a small number of “best products” pages.

It creates:

* repetitive user journeys,
* diluted relevance,
* and weaker topical alignment.

### Generic sitewide CTAs

A support article about “best pillows for neck pain” should not rely on a generic sidebar button saying:

* “Shop top deals”.

## The most effective affiliate sites behave like guided decision systems

The strongest affiliate websites do not treat internal links as isolated SEO elements.

They treat them as guided transitions between stages of decision-making.

A support page succeeds when it leaves the reader better prepared for the next commercial step. A money page succeeds when it resolves the next buying decision with minimal friction.

Support-to-money mapping is therefore not just a linking exercise. It is the mechanism that turns informational traffic into commercially useful journeys.

When the mapping is accurate:

* readers move naturally through the site,
* commercial pages receive better-qualified traffic,
* [comparison pages]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-comparison-5755f1/' | relative_url }}) convert more effectively,
* and affiliate revenue becomes less dependent on aggressive selling.

The result is usually a site that feels more helpful to users while also producing stronger revenue per visitor over time.
