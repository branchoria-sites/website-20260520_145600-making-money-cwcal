## Introduction

A strong affiliate money page does more than list products and display affiliate buttons. It acts as a navigation system that helps readers move from uncertainty to a buying route that matches their situation. The page becomes a commercial hub: a place where visitors sort themselves by budget, feature priority, use case, brand preference, experience level, or problem type before clicking through to a merchant.


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_internal_l_d8b176_money_pages_navigati_58571f-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Money Hubs illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_internal_l_d8b176_money_pages_navigati_58571f-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_internal_l_d8b176_money_pages_navigati_58571f-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
This matters because many visitors arrive with incomplete buying intent. Someone searching for “best air fryer” may still be deciding between basket and oven styles, compact versus family-sized models, or cheap versus premium options. A hub-style money page helps narrow those decisions instead of forcing every reader towards the same recommendation. That improves user flow, increases trust, and often raises [affiliate revenue]({{ 'untitled/revenue-leaks/' | relative_url }}) per visitor because readers reach more relevant offers.

Google’s guidance on [internal linking]({{ 'untitled/internal-linking/' | relative_url }}) and ecommerce structure repeatedly emphasises that links and navigation help search engines understand page relationships and importance. It also stresses the value of descriptive [anchor text]({{ 'untitled/anchor-text/' | relative_url }}) that helps users predict where a link leads. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[Google for Developers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search CentralLearn how to make your links crawlable so that Google can...</span></span></span> For affiliate sites, that same structure affects conversion paths just as much as rankings.

## Why money pages are not just affiliate-button pages

Weak affiliate pages often behave like dead ends. They present a short list of products, insert “buy now” buttons, and give readers few ways to refine their choice. This creates a mismatch between visitor intent and product recommendations.

A navigational money page behaves differently. It acknowledges that readers arrive at different stages of decision-making and need routes through the page.

Instead of acting like a single recommendation page, the hub acts more like a controlled junction:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* Readers with low budgets move towards “best under £100” sections.
* Readers with specific constraints move towards “best for small kitchens” or “best for pets”.
* Readers comparing brands move into brand-versus-brand pages.
* Readers still learning move back into supporting educational content.
* Readers ready to buy reach specific review or retailer pages.

</div>

This structure mirrors what usability researchers call “information scent”: the idea that users follow links that appear likely to move them closer to their goal. Nielsen Norman Group describes information scent as the cues users use to decide where to click next. Weak or misleading scent causes abandonment and backtracking. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-scent/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nngroup.com">[nngroup.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nngroup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">information scent</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How Users Decide Where to Go Next2 Feb 2020 — When deciding which links to click on the web, users choose those with the highest informat...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wrong-information-scent-costs-sales/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nngroup.com">[nngroup.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nngroup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">wrong information scent costs sales</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Deceivingly Strong Information Scent Costs Sales1 Aug 2004 — Information scent refers to the extent to which users can predict what they...</span></span></span>

On affiliate sites, strong information scent usually means links that clearly communicate buying relevance:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* “Best cordless vacuums for pet hair”
* “Quiet dehumidifiers for bedrooms”
* “Compare Ninja versus Cosori air fryers”
* “Best budget espresso machines under £300”

</div>

These routes are commercially valuable because they narrow intent. The reader feels closer to a purchase decision after every click.

A page full of vague calls to action such as “read more”, “learn more”, or “see options” creates weaker scent and weaker commercial progression. Google also recommends descriptive anchor text because it helps both users and search engines understand linked pages. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[Google for Developers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search CentralLearn how to make your links crawlable so that Google can...</span></span></span>

## How to route readers by use case or buying constraint

The most effective money hubs organise products around real buying decisions rather than arbitrary lists. This is especially important for affiliate sites targeting broad commercial keywords with mixed intent.

### Use-case routing

Use-case routing divides readers according to the practical problem they want solved.

For example, a “best standing desks” hub may branch into:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* Best for dual-monitor setups
* Best for tall users
* Best for small flats
* Best for gaming
* Best budget standing desks
* Best premium hardwood desks

</div>

Each branch represents a different commercial path with different products, retailers, pricing levels, and conversion rates.

This structure matters because broad comparison keywords often contain several hidden sub-intents. Treating all visitors identically usually reduces click-through rates and buyer confidence.

A scalable affiliate system can identify these recurring patterns across niches:

Commercial modifierReader intent“best budget”price-sensitive“best premium”feature-sensitive“best for beginners”reassurance-sensitive“best for small spaces”constraint-sensitive“quiet”, “portable”, “lightweight”lifestyle-sensitive“vs”comparison-sensitive

These modifiers frequently become the internal routes inside hub-style money pages.

### Budget segmentation

Budget segmentation is one of the strongest navigational systems because many affiliate categories naturally divide into pricing tiers.

A good money hub does not simply rank products from cheapest to most expensive. Instead, it helps readers self-select into realistic purchase bands.

For example:

* Under £50
* £50–£150
* Mid-range
* Premium
* Commercial-grade

This improves conversion quality because readers stop evaluating products they were unlikely to buy anyway.

It also creates scalable internal-linking opportunities: <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://gr0.com/blog/internal-linking-best-practices" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gr0.com">[gr0.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gr0.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">internal linking best practices</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internal Linking And Anchor Text Best Practices13 Dec 2023 — Internal linking and anchor text are key practices for SEO. <div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* “Best office chairs” links into “Best ergonomic chairs under £200”.
* “Premium ergonomic chairs” links into detailed reviews of high-ticket products.
* Budget pages link into accessory or upgrade pages later in the user journey.

</div>

For affiliate operators, this structure can increase average commission value while still serving lower-intent visitors appropriately.

### Feature-led navigation

Some niches convert better when organised by feature priority rather than generic rankings.

Examples include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Cameras organised by autofocus, portability, or low-light performance
* Hosting platforms organised by speed, beginner-friendliness, or ecommerce support
* Coffee machines organised by milk frothing, bean grinding, or maintenance simplicity

</div>

This matters because feature-driven searches often signal stronger commercial intent than broad informational traffic.

A visitor searching for “best blender for frozen fruit” is closer to purchase than someone searching “how blenders work”.

Hub-style money pages can exploit this by linking feature sections directly into matching reviews and retailer paths.

## Hub pages reduce commercial dead ends

One major affiliate-site failure is stranded informational traffic. Readers land on helpful articles but never reach commercially useful pages.

Hub-style money pages help solve this by becoming central routing points between educational and transactional content.

For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. A reader lands on “How much humidity should a bedroom have?”
2. Internal links point towards “quiet dehumidifiers for bedrooms”.
3. The money hub then routes readers into:

</div>
budget models
low-energy models
smart dehumidifiers
detailed product reviews
4. [Review pages]({{ 'untitled/review-pages/' | relative_url }}) contain merchant links.

The hub acts as a conversion bridge rather than expecting a single article to perform every role.

Google notes that navigation structures and cross-page links influence how it understands ecommerce site structure and relationships between pages. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[Google for Developers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search CentralLearn how to make your links crawlable so that Google can...</span></span></span> Affiliate sites benefit from the same principle because readers also interpret site structure through links.

A well-designed money hub therefore improves two things simultaneously:

* search-engine understanding of topical relationships
* reader progression towards commercial intent

## The commercial advantage of multiple next steps

A common mistake is designing money pages with only one intended action: clicking an affiliate button.

In practice, many users are not ready for that step immediately. They still need reassurance, narrowing, or comparison.

Hub-style money pages increase revenue partly because they preserve momentum instead of forcing premature exits.

Useful “next-step” routes include:

* detailed product reviews
* [comparison pages]({{ 'untitled/comparison-pages/' | relative_url }})
* brand-versus-brand pages
* “best for” sub-pages <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[developers.google.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search CentralLearn how to make your links crawlable so that Google can...</span></span></span>
* setup or ownership guides
* accessory pages
* deal or discount pages

This creates a wider commercial funnel inside the site itself.

For example, a reader comparing espresso machines may not click a retailer link immediately. But they may click:

* “Breville versus DeLonghi”

* pages per session
* affiliate click depth
* conversion likelihood

## Weak hub structures that reduce affiliate revenue

Several common mistakes reduce the effectiveness of money pages as navigational hubs.

### Overloaded comparison pages

Some affiliate pages try to compare too many products at once without meaningful grouping.

* 25 similar products
* weak categorisation
* little filtering guidance
* repetitive summaries

Readers struggle to identify which route fits them.

Segmented hubs usually perform better because they simplify decisions.
