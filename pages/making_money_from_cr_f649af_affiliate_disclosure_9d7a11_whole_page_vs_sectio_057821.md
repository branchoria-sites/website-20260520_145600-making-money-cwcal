## Introduction

UK affiliate sites do not all need the same [disclosure]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-review-pag-f66da7-affiliate-disclosure-8b10cd/' | relative_url }}) treatment. A page built almost entirely to push affiliate-linked products is usually closer to a full advertising page, while a broader guide with a few monetised recommendations may only need clearly marked commercial sections. That distinction matters for both compliance and long-term monetisation.


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_disclosure_9d7a11_whole_page_vs_sectio_057821-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Page labels illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_disclosure_9d7a11_whole_page_vs_sectio_057821-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_disclosure_9d7a11_whole_page_vs_sectio_057821-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
The Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) and CAP guidance focus on whether readers can recognise advertising before engaging with it. At the same time, Google’s spam policies focus on whether affiliate pages add real value beyond sending users to [merchants]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-best-of-affiliate-pa-55feea-multiple-merchant-li-625ce1/' | relative_url }}). Together, those pressures create an important design decision for affiliate websites: should the entire page be treated as commercial content, or should only specific recommendation modules carry advertising labels? <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/news/get-yourself-affiliated-with-the-rules-on-affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">get yourself affiliated with the rules on affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAGet yourself affiliated with the rules on affiliate marketing10 Oct 2024 — This CAP Advice explains the circumstances under which diff...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Online Affiliate Marketing - ASA22 Mar 2023 — Affiliate marketing is a type of performance-based marketing where an affiliate is rewarded...</span></span></span>

For scalable website systems, this is not just a [wording]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-disclosure-9d7a11-clear-affiliate-word-ac25e8/' | relative_url }}) issue. It affects page templates, content classification, internal-linking logic, comparison depth, and conversion layout. Sites that get the distinction wrong often end up with pages that look deceptively editorial to users while also looking thin and repetitive to search engines.

## When a roundup effectively becomes an advert

Some affiliate pages are commercial from the first line. A “best laptops under £500” page containing ten affiliate-linked products, merchant buttons, price [tables]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-best-of-affiliate-pa-55feea-comparison-tables-cl-5eb30a/' | relative_url }}), and buying prompts is not functioning like neutral editorial coverage in practice. Even if written in an article format, the commercial purpose dominates the page.

CAP guidance increasingly treats these situations as requiring upfront recognition that the content is advertising. The ASA notes that where content closely resembles editorial material but exists mainly to promote affiliate-linked products, labels such as “Ad” may need to appear before users engage with the article itself. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-native-advertising.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">recognising ads native advertising</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASARecognising ads: Native advertising23 Mar 2023 — “Native” advertising is content that has been designed so it doesn&#x27;t look out of plac...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/static/2882373b-162e-4a1c-82e5806479d152cd/Affiliate-Infographic.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK advertising rules and affiliate marketingADVICE. Historically, many affiliate publishers tried to avoid explicit page-level labels because they feared lower click-through rates. Older affiliate SEO models often depended on mimicking magazine reviews or independent recommendations as closely as possible. That became harder to defend as regulators focused more heavily on “native” advertising formats that blend into editorial environments. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/news/get-yourself-affiliated-with-the-rules-on-affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">get yourself affiliated with the rules on affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAGet yourself affiliated with the rules on affiliate marketing10 Oct 2024 — This CAP Advice explains the circumstances under which diff...</span></span></span>

In practice, a page increasingly behaves like a full-page advert when most of these conditions apply:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The primary purpose is product referral rather than education.
* Nearly every outbound link is monetised.
* Product blocks dominate the page layout.
* The page has commercial-intent search targeting such as “best”, “top”, “cheap”, or “discount”.
* The article structure exists mainly to support affiliate clicks.
* There is little non-commercial utility once affiliate links are removed.

</div>

That does not make the page unusable or non-compliant. Many successful affiliate businesses openly operate commercial [comparison pages]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-comparison-5755f1/' | relative_url }}). The issue is whether the page honestly signals what it is.

For a website-generation system, this creates a useful classification rule: highly monetised comparison pages should usually default to page-level disclosure rather than relying on isolated link disclosures buried lower down.

A strong page-level disclosure model also simplifies scaling. Instead of manually deciding disclosure placement for every comparison article, the system can automatically apply a prominent disclosure banner to all pages in selected commercial templates:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* “Best X” roundups
* Product-vs-product comparisons
* Deal pages
* Voucher pages
* Seasonal shopping guides
* “Top rated” commercial lists

</div>

This reduces legal inconsistency across large content inventories.

## Why mixed editorial pages are different

Not every affiliate page needs to look like a giant advert notice. A broader informational guide can contain affiliate-linked recommendations without the entire page being treated as fully commercial.

This distinction matters because many high-performing affiliate sites monetise informational traffic rather than purely transactional traffic. A long guide about improving sleep, setting up a home office, or starting hiking in winter may contain valuable non-commercial advice alongside selected monetised recommendations.

In these mixed-content situations, CAP guidance allows for marked advertising sections or clearly identified affiliate-linked areas rather than requiring the whole article to be labelled as advertising. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Online Affiliate Marketing - ASA22 Mar 2023 — Affiliate marketing is a type of performance-based marketing where an affiliate is rewarded...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-native-advertising.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">recognising ads native advertising</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASARecognising ads: Native advertising23 Mar 2023 — “Native” advertising is content that has been designed so it doesn&#x27;t look out of plac...</span></span></span>

The practical difference is intent balance.

A mixed informational page usually still delivers standalone value without requiring a purchase. Readers can complete much of their task from the content itself. The affiliate blocks support the advice rather than replacing it.

For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A guide to preventing back pain while working remotely may include one recommended office chair section.
* A camping tutorial may contain a marked “Recommended gear” module.
* A photography explainer may include a clearly labelled “Suggested beginner cameras” block.

</div>

In these cases, module-level disclosure is often more proportionate than treating the entire article as an advert.

This creates a different monetisation pattern from pure affiliate roundups. The page’s value comes from informational trust first, with [affiliate conversion]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-conversion-fac650/' | relative_url }}) added later in the user flow.

That distinction has important revenue implications:

## How section-level disclosure changes page design

Once a site uses module-level disclosure instead of whole-page labelling, page structure becomes much more important.

Readers should immediately understand where editorial discussion ends and commercial recommendation begins. The clearer the visual separation, the easier it becomes to preserve trust while still monetising informational traffic.

This is why many modern affiliate publishers isolate commercial areas using:

* “Recommended products” boxes
* [Comparison tables]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-best-of-affiliate-pa-55feea-comparison-tables-cl-5eb30a/' | relative_url }}) with disclosure headers

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Separate shopping modules
* Distinct background colours or borders
* Labels such as “Affiliate picks”, “Ad”, or “Paid links”

</div>

The goal is not merely legal protection. It also improves behavioural clarity.

Users increasingly recognise affiliate monetisation patterns. When commercial blocks are clearly separated, visitors are less likely to feel manipulated after clicking through to merchants. That can improve repeat traffic and brand trust over time.

From a systems perspective, module-level disclosure also supports reusable monetisation components. A website-generation system can insert standardised affiliate modules into broader informational content without converting every article into a fully commercial page.

That matters operationally because informational content often scales differently from high-intent buyer pages.

For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* One central educational guide may feed traffic into multiple affiliate modules.
* Different affiliate modules can be swapped without rewriting the article.
* Commercial density can be tested independently of informational content.
* Internal links can move users from informational pages into more aggressively commercial pages later in the funnel.

</div>

This creates cleaner monetisation architecture than blending affiliate links throughout paragraphs with inconsistent disclosure.

## The thin-content problem gets worse when disclosure is vague

Poor disclosure and thin affiliate content often reinforce each other.

A low-value affiliate page frequently tries to hide both problems simultaneously:

* the lack of original insight
* the commercial intent

Google’s spam guidance specifically warns against affiliate pages that add little original value beyond duplicated merchant information. ## Template rules for scalable affiliate sites

A scalable affiliate publishing system benefits from fixed [disclosure rules]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-disclosure-9d7a11/' | relative_url }}) tied to page type rather than manual judgement.

The most effective systems usually separate pages into commercial-intent tiers.

### Tier 1: Fully commercial affiliate pages

These pages are primarily designed to drive affiliate conversions.

Typical examples:

* “Best wireless earbuds”
* “Top mattresses for side sleepers”
* “Cheapest VPN deals”
* “Best credit card rewards”

Recommended approach:

* prominent page-level disclosure near the title
* commercial intent obvious immediately
* standardised comparison layouts
* clear affiliate explanation before product interaction

These pages should also contain substantial original filtering logic, recommendation reasoning, or comparison value to avoid thin-affiliate patterns.

## Why the distinction affects revenue per visitor

There is a common misconception that weaker disclosure always increases earnings because fewer users notice the affiliate relationship.

In practice, the opposite can happen over time.

Pages that openly acknowledge commercial intent often perform better on:

* repeat visits
* newsletter signups
* brand recall
* return search traffic
* long-session engagement

Users are increasingly familiar with affiliate monetisation. Hidden monetisation patterns tend to create distrust when discovered, especially on pages pretending to be purely editorial.

There is also a conversion-path difference between whole-page commercial intent and mixed-content monetisation.

A fully commercial roundup usually targets users close to purchase. These pages may convert strongly but compete heavily and face greater thin-content pressure.

Mixed advice pages often monetise earlier-stage visitors:

* research traffic
* problem-solving traffic
* educational traffic
* comparison-stage users

These users may convert later through internal links or repeat visits rather than immediate clicks.

That makes disclosure structure part of funnel design rather than just compliance wording.

A mature affiliate system therefore benefits from using both models deliberately:

* page-level disclosure for overtly commercial comparison content
* module-level disclosure for broader informational ecosystems

The key is consistency. Readers should never have to guess where the commercial relationship begins.
