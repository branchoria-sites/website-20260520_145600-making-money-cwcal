## Introduction

Revenue per visitor (RPV) is one of the most useful commercial metrics for affiliate websites because it measures how much approved commission a page generates for each visitor who lands on it. Unlike traffic, rankings, or raw affiliate clicks, RPV connects the entire monetisation chain into a single number. It shows whether a page attracts commercially valuable visitors and whether the offer, merchant, and page structure convert that intent into actual earnings.


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_conversion_fac650_affiliate_rpv_formul_1bec21-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="RPV formula illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_conversion_fac650_affiliate_rpv_formul_1bec21-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_conversion_fac650_affiliate_rpv_formul_1bec21-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
For affiliate pages, the core calculation is simple:

RPV=Approved Affiliate RevenueVisitorsRPV = \frac{\text{Approved [Affiliate Revenue]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-conversion-fac650-affiliate-revenue-le-8c2b2f/' | relative_url }})&#125;&#125;{\text{Visitors&#125;&#125;RPV=VisitorsApproved Affiliate Revenue​

But useful affiliate RPV analysis goes further than a basic division formula. A commercially accurate calculation must include click-through rate, merchant conversion rate, commission value, and approval rate. That turns RPV from a vanity metric into a decision-making tool for choosing [page types]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-buyer-intent-revenue-fe10e4-affiliate-page-types-956d68/' | relative_url }}), affiliate offers, internal links, and expansion opportunities across a site.

## Why traffic and clicks are incomplete signals

Affiliate sites often overvalue traffic growth because traffic is easy to measure. A page with 50,000 monthly visitors can still earn less than a page with 2,000 visitors if the larger page attracts weak commercial intent or sends visitors to poorly converting [merchants]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-best-of-affiliate-pa-55feea-multiple-merchant-li-625ce1/' | relative_url }}).

Clicks alone are also misleading. Affiliate dashboards frequently emphasise EPC (earnings per click), which measures average earnings generated from affiliate clicks. Post Affiliate Pro <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://partnerstack.com/glossary/earnings-per-click-epc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: partnerstack.com">[PartnerStack EPC is useful]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">partnerstack.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Earnings Per Click (EPC) Definition &amp; MeaningEarnings per click (EPC) is a key performance indicator in affiliate marketing that measures...</span></span></span>, but it only measures value after the visitor has already clicked an affiliate link. It does not tell you how efficiently the page itself converts visitors into commercially useful actions.

A page can therefore have:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* High traffic but weak buying intent
* High click-through rate but poor merchant conversion
* Strong sales volume but low approval rates
* High commission percentages but low average order values

</div>

RPV combines these factors into one commercially meaningful metric.

For affiliate website systems, this matters because scaling decisions should be based on revenue efficiency, not raw visibility. A template that earns 18p per visitor is usually more valuable than one that earns 2p per visitor, even if the lower-performing page receives more traffic.

This distinction changes how successful page types are identified. A broad informational article may help [internal linking]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-internal-l-d8b176/' | relative_url }}) and topical coverage, but a tightly focused “best”, “comparison”, or “alternatives” page may generate far higher RPV because the user is already close to purchase intent.

## The four numbers needed for revenue per visitor

A practical affiliate RPV model can be broken into four connected metrics:

RPV=CTR×MerchantConversionRate×AverageCommission×ApprovalRateRPV = CTR \times Merchant\ Conversion\ Rate \times Average\ Commission \times Approval\ RateRPV=CTR×MerchantConversionRate×AverageCommission×ApprovalRate

Each part explains a different stage of the monetisation funnel.

### Affiliate click-through rate

Click-through rate (CTR) measures how many visitors click an affiliate link after landing on the page.

Example:

* 1,000 visitors
* 120 affiliate clicks <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://partnerstack.com/glossary/earnings-per-click-epc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: partnerstack.com">[partnerstack.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">partnerstack.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Earnings Per Click (EPC) Definition &amp; MeaningEarnings per click (EPC) is a key performance indicator in affiliate marketing that measures...</span></span></span>

CTR:

CTR=1201000=12%CTR = \frac{120}{1000} = 12\%CTR=1000120​=12%

This number reflects how effectively the page moves users from information to action.

CTR is heavily influenced by:

* Query intent
* Offer placement
* Product relevance
* Comparison clarity
* Mobile usability
* Trust signals
* Call-to-action [wording]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-disclosure-9d7a11-clear-affiliate-word-ac25e8/' | relative_url }})

A page answering “best cordless lawn mower for small gardens” will usually produce a higher affiliate CTR than a broad educational article about lawn care because the visitor arrives closer to a buying decision.

For scalable affiliate systems, CTR often becomes a template-level variable. If one comparison-table layout consistently improves affiliate clicks across multiple pages, that structure can be reused site-wide.

### Merchant conversion rate

Merchant conversion rate measures how many affiliate clicks become completed actions such as sales or approved leads.

Example:

* 120 clicks sent
* 6 sales completed

Merchant conversion rate:

MerchantConversionRate=6120=5%Merchant\ Conversion\ Rate = \frac{6}{120} = 5\%MerchantConversionRate=1206​=5%

This is where many affiliate publishers lose revenue visibility. They optimise page clicks without considering what happens after the visitor leaves the site.

Merchant-side factors strongly affect RPV:

* Regional compatibility

</div>

### Average commission value

Average commission value measures how much approved revenue is generated from each successful conversion.

Example:

* 6 approved sales
* £180 total commission

Average commission:

AverageCommission=£1806=£30Average\ Commission = \frac{£180}{6} = £30AverageCommission=6£180​=£30

This is affected by:

* Product price
* Commission percentage
* Flat-fee payouts
* Upsells
* Subscription renewals
* Basket size

Higher commission rates do not automatically produce better RPV.

A merchant paying 12% commission on low-converting products may earn less overall than a merchant paying 4% commission with excellent checkout conversion and strong average order values.

This is why affiliate [offer selection]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-offer-sele-904ed4/' | relative_url }}) should be evaluated using complete RPV data rather than commission percentages alone.

### Approval rate

Approval rate measures how much tracked commission survives fraud checks, cancellations, returns, or rejected leads.

Example:

* £180 tracked commission
* £144 approved commission

Approval rate:

ApprovalRate=£144£180=80%Approval\ Rate = \frac{£144}{£180} = 80\%ApprovalRate=£180£144​=80%

Many affiliate publishers ignore this stage and overestimate page profitability.

Low approval rates commonly occur in:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* Finance lead generation
* Incentivised traffic
* Coupon-heavy verticals
* Poor-quality lead funnels
* Aggressive advertorial pages

</div>

For scalable affiliate operations, approval-rate analysis is critical because apparently profitable pages can collapse after reversals and rejected commissions are applied.

A page earning £500 tracked revenue with a 40% approval rate is commercially weaker than a page earning £350 tracked revenue with a 95% approval rate.

## Worked examples for judging page value

### Example 1: High traffic but weak RPV

A broad informational page receives:

* 20,000 visitors
* 3% affiliate CTR
* 2% merchant conversion rate
* £12 average commission
* 85% approval rate

</div>

Calculation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* 20,000 visitors
* 600 affiliate clicks <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://partnerstack.com/glossary/earnings-per-click-epc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: partnerstack.com">[partnerstack.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">partnerstack.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Earnings Per Click (EPC) Definition &amp; MeaningEarnings per click (EPC) is a key performance indicator in affiliate marketing that measures...</span></span></span>
* 12 conversions
* £144 raw commission
* £122.40 approved commission

</div>

RPV:

RPV=£122.4020000=£0.00612RPV = \frac{£122.40}{20000} = £0.00612RPV=20000£122.40​=£0.00612

The page earns roughly 0.6p per visitor.

Despite strong traffic, commercial efficiency is poor.

This commonly happens with educational queries that attract curiosity rather than purchase intent.

## How RPV changes content decisions

Once affiliate pages are judged by RPV rather than page views, content strategy changes substantially.

### Topic selection becomes commercially filtered

Some topics attract large audiences but weak monetisation intent. Others attract fewer visitors but much stronger buying behaviour.

An affiliate system built around RPV learns to prioritise:

* Product comparisons
* “Best for” queries
* Alternatives pages
* Pricing pages
* Buyer-decision content
* Commercial problem-solving queries

rather than purely informational traffic.

### Internal linking becomes revenue-focused

Internal links should not only distribute authority. They should move users towards higher-RPV pages.

Examples include:

* Informational pages linking into [comparison pages]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-comparison-5755f1/' | relative_url }})
* Troubleshooting pages linking into replacement-product pages
* Review pages linking into merchant comparisons
* “How it works” articles linking into buying guides

This creates monetisation funnels inside the site architecture itself.

### Offer testing becomes measurable

Without RPV tracking, changing affiliate offers becomes guesswork.

With RPV analysis, publishers can test:

* Different merchants
* Different comparison-table layouts
* Link positions
* Call-to-action wording
* Mobile button placement
* Product ordering

against actual visitor value rather than clicks alone.

A merchant with lower commission percentages may still produce higher RPV if checkout conversion and approval rates are substantially better.

## Why approved revenue matters more than reported revenue

Many affiliate dashboards initially report pending or tracked commission rather than final approved earnings.

Using unapproved revenue inflates RPV calculations and can produce misleading optimisation decisions.

The safest calculation uses:

* Final approved commissions
* Real unique visitors
* Matched date windows
* Consistent attribution periods

This matters especially in industries with long approval cycles or high refund rates.

For example:

* Software trials may convert weeks later
* Financial products may require compliance approval
* Ecommerce orders may be cancelled or returned
* Subscription products may claw back commissions after refunds

A page that appears profitable early can become commercially weak after reversals are processed.

For scalable affiliate systems, delayed approval behaviour should therefore be considered part of page performance modelling rather than treated as an accounting afterthought.

## RPV is ultimately a page-quality metric

Revenue per visitor is not only a finance metric. It is a page-quality metric because it measures how effectively a page aligns intent, offer selection, merchant quality, and conversion flow.

High-RPV pages usually share common traits:

* Strong intent matching
* Clear commercial framing
* Relevant affiliate offers
* Trustworthy recommendations
* Fast user movement towards decisions
* Good mobile usability
* Effective internal linking
* Reliable merchants

This makes RPV one of the most useful signals for deciding which affiliate page patterns deserve expansion across a website portfolio.

Traffic explains reach. Clicks explain engagement. Revenue per visitor explains whether the page actually earns.
