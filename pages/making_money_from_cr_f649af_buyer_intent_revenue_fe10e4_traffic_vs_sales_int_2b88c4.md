## Introduction

A large affiliate website can attract hundreds of thousands of visitors and still produce disappointing revenue if most of its traffic comes from readers who are only looking for explanations, definitions, or basic guidance. The key distinction is not simply traffic volume. It is whether the visitor is close enough to a buying decision that affiliate links match the intent of the page.

In practice, informational traffic and affiliate sales traffic behave differently. Informational visitors often want answers, tutorials, or background knowledge. Commercial visitors are comparing options, checking whether a product is worth buying, narrowing a shortlist, or looking for reassurance before spending money. Commercial and transactional searches consistently convert better because the user is already moving toward action. For affiliate websites, separating these two traffic types is one of the most important monetisation decisions. It affects which topics deserve dedicated pages, where affiliate links should appear, which pages should prioritise email capture instead of product offers, and how [internal linking]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-internal-l-d8b176/' | relative_url }}) moves readers from research into purchase-ready comparisons.

## Why informational traffic often earns poorly

Informational traffic is usually top-of-funnel traffic. The visitor has a question but not yet a buying decision.

Examples include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* “How does an air fryer work”
* “What is cloud storage”
* “Why is my lawn yellow”
* “How much protein do runners need”

</div>

These searches can attract large volumes because broad educational questions naturally have wide audiences. However, they often produce weak affiliate performance because the visitor is not yet evaluating products.

A page answering “what is VPN encryption” may receive more traffic than a page targeting “best VPN for streaming BBC iPlayer abroad”, yet the second page is far more likely to generate clicks and sales because the user is already solving a purchase problem.

This distinction matters because [affiliate revenue]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-conversion-fac650-affiliate-revenue-le-8c2b2f/' | relative_url }}) depends on measurable actions. For large-scale website systems, this creates a practical rule:

* informational traffic is useful for audience acquisition and topical authority
* commercial investigation traffic is usually where direct affiliate revenue appears

Treating both traffic types as equally valuable leads to distorted topic selection and poor revenue-per-visitor performance.

## Informational searches still matter, but they need lighter monetisation

Informational traffic is not useless. In many niches it provides the scale, trust, and topical breadth needed for the commercial pages to rank.

The mistake is assuming informational pages should monetise in the same way as [comparison pages]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-comparison-5755f1/' | relative_url }}).

A tutorial page about “how to clean leather boots” may support affiliate revenue indirectly by:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* introducing readers to the site
* building topical authority
* generating return visits
* feeding users into buying guides
* capturing email subscribers
* creating internal-link pathways toward commercial pages

</div>

But aggressively monetising that page with repeated affiliate buttons often harms both user experience and conversion efficiency.

Informational pages usually work better with lighter monetisation patterns such as:

* a single contextual product mention
* subtle [comparison tables]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-best-of-affiliate-pa-55feea-comparison-tables-cl-5eb30a/' | relative_url }})

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* “recommended tools” blocks
* internal links into commercial guides
* email capture tied to the topic

</div>

For example:

> “If your boots are cracking because of dry leather, our guide to the best leather conditioners compares the products that restore flexibility without darkening the material.”

That internal transition moves the visitor from education into product evaluation naturally.

This distinction becomes especially important for high-volume website systems. A scalable affiliate site should not apply the same template to every page. Informational pages and commercial pages require different layouts, different conversion goals, and different link density.

## Commercial investigation pages deserve stronger offer placement

Commercial pages exist to help the reader make a decision. The visitor expects products, comparisons, rankings, specifications, and recommendations.

That changes what works on the page.

A commercial investigation page can support:

* prominent affiliate buttons
* comparison [tables]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-best-of-affiliate-pa-55feea-comparison-tables-cl-5eb30a/' | relative_url }})

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--benefit" markdown="1">

* “best overall” selections
* pricing sections
* merchant comparisons
* pros-and-cons summaries
* conversion-focused layouts

</div>

The reader is already in evaluation mode, so the affiliate offer aligns with intent instead of interrupting it.

For example, someone searching:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* “best cordless vacuum for pet hair”
* “Surfshark vs NordVPN”
* “best running shoes for flat feet”

</div>

is actively filtering options before buying.

Those pages should therefore prioritise:

1. rapid product comparison
2. trust-building [evidence]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-best-of-affiliate-pa-55feea-evidence-blocks-affi-b0d439/' | relative_url }})
3. clear recommendations
4. merchant clicks

This is where affiliate monetisation becomes most efficient because the page intent and commercial action are aligned.

## Internal links are what turn learning traffic into buying traffic

The highest-performing affiliate sites often use informational pages as feeders into commercial pages rather than treating them as isolated articles.

Affiliate systems that connect those stages effectively can monetise both discovery traffic and buying traffic without forcing every page into a hard-sell format.

## Common monetisation mistakes

Several recurring mistakes blur the line between informational and affiliate sales traffic.

### Treating every page as a review page

Many affiliate sites force product recommendations into pages where the reader is not yet product-aware.

Examples include:

* beginner glossary pages
* broad educational tutorials
* historical explainers
* technical definitions

This often reduces trust and weakens user satisfaction because the monetisation appears disconnected from the query.

### Ignoring hybrid intent

Some searches contain both educational and commercial behaviour.

For example:

* “what size dehumidifier do I need”
* “how much protein powder should I take”
* “which type of office chair is best for posture”

These searches often support hybrid pages:

* educational opening sections
* buying [criteria]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-comparison-5755f1-category-specific-cr-a92f21/' | relative_url }}) explanations
* product recommendations later in the page

Hybrid intent pages are often some of the highest-value affiliate assets because they capture users transitioning from confusion into evaluation.

### Chasing traffic instead of revenue density

Large informational keywords can look attractive because of search volume.

But revenue density matters more.

A site receiving:

* 20,000 visits at £0.02 revenue per visitor

can earn less than:

* 2,000 visits at £1 revenue per visitor

This is why affiliate operators often expand commercial keyword clusters first once a niche proves profitable.
