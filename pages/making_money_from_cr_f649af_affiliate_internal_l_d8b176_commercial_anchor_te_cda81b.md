## Introduction

Anchor text is the short piece of clickable text that tells a reader what happens after the click. On affiliate websites, that tiny decision often determines whether visitors continue towards a comparison page, product review, or buying guide — or leave the site entirely.


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_internal_l_d8b176_commercial_anchor_te_cda81b-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Anchor Text illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_internal_l_d8b176_commercial_anchor_te_cda81b-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_internal_l_d8b176_commercial_anchor_te_cda81b-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
Weak anchor text wastes commercial intent. Links such as “read more”, “click here”, or “best options” force readers to guess what they will get next. Strong anchor text reduces uncertainty. It explains the destination clearly, matches the reader’s stage in the buying journey, and makes the next step feel useful rather than promotional.

Google’s own guidance says good anchor text should be descriptive, concise, and relevant to the destination page. It should set expectations for readers and help search engines understand what the linked page covers. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[Google for Developers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search CentralGood anchor text is descriptive, reasonably concise, and r...</span></span></span>

For affiliate sites, this matters because internal links are not just navigational elements. They are conversion paths. The [wording]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-disclosure-9d7a11-clear-affiliate-word-ac25e8/' | relative_url }}) of the link can change whether an informational visitor progresses towards a money page or stalls inside the research phase.

## Why vague anchors lose clicks

Generic anchor text fails because it removes “information scent” — the immediate clue that tells readers whether a click is worth their time. Nielsen Norman Group repeatedly highlights that vague labels such as “learn more” or “click here” create poor usability because users cannot predict the destination confidently. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/writing-links/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nngroup.com">[nngroup.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nngroup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">writing links</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Even when the links visually stand out, they need to be meaningful to be helpful. This problem becomes expensive on affiliate websites because readers often arrive with partial intent. They may already know the problem but still need help narrowing choices. At that stage, uncertainty kills movement.

Consider this example from a supporting article about cordless lawn mowers:

* Weak anchor: “Read more” <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[developers.google.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search CentralGood anchor text is descriptive, reasonably concise, and r...</span></span></span>
* Strong anchor: “Compare the best cordless lawn mowers for small gardens”

The second version answers three silent questions immediately:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* What type of page is this?
* What problem does it solve?
* Is it relevant to me?

</div>

The stronger version sells the next step because it previews the reward after the click.

### Generic anchors weaken commercial pathways

Many affiliate sites accidentally hide their money pages behind meaningless links:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* “View options”
* “Check products”
* “More information”
* “See reviews”
* “Continue reading”

</div>

These anchors create friction because they force users to interpret context manually. On content-heavy sites with large article libraries, vague links also make pages blend together.

That creates three monetisation problems:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. Readers skip links because the destination feels uncertain.
2. Search engines receive weaker topical signals about the linked page.
3. Commercial pages receive less qualified traffic.

</div>

Google specifically advises against overly generic anchor text because it does not provide enough context. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[Google for Developers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search CentralGood anchor text is descriptive, reasonably concise, and r...</span></span></span>

For affiliate systems at scale, this becomes a compounding issue. A site with 5,000 informational pages and weak anchors may technically link to money pages everywhere, yet still fail to move users into comparison content effectively.

## How to describe the destination page clearly

Good commercial anchor text behaves like a miniature promise. It tells readers exactly what type of page they will reach and why that page matters.

The most effective anchors usually combine three elements:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* The destination format
* The commercial intent
* The reader’s specific situation

</div>

For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* “Best running watches for beginners”
* “Air fryer comparison chart”
* “iPhone 16 battery life review”
* “Cheapest VPN deals for streaming”
* “Compare portable monitors under £200”

</div>

These anchors work because they align tightly with user intent.

### Name the page type directly

<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://mangools.com/blog/anchor-text/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mangools.com">[Readers respond better when anchor text identifies the format of the destination page.]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mangools.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">anchor text</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What Is Anchor Text &amp; Why It Matters for SEO?18 Apr 2021 — Anchor text is a clickable piece of text that serves as a link from one page t...</span></span></span>

Useful commercial formats include:

* Reviews
* Comparisons
* Buying guides
* Deals
* Rankings
* Setup guides
* Alternatives
* Product roundups

Examples:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* “Read our Shark vs Dyson comparison”
* “See the full standing desk buying guide”
* “Compare the best budget espresso machines”
* “Check today’s mattress deals”

</div>

This matters because different [page types]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-buyer-intent-revenue-fe10e4-affiliate-page-types-956d68/' | relative_url }}) satisfy different levels of purchase intent.

Someone researching “how loud are portable air conditioners” may not be ready for a direct affiliate link, but they may willingly click:

* “Compare the quietest portable air conditioners”

The anchor turns an informational visitor into a comparison visitor without forcing the transition.

### Match the reader’s stage of intent

Commercial anchors perform best when they reflect where the user currently is in the decision process.

A mismatch reduces clicks.

For example, a beginner-focused article about espresso basics should not suddenly push readers towards:

* “Buy the Breville Barista Touch”

That leap feels premature.

A better transition would be:

* “Compare beginner-friendly espresso machines”

The anchor respects the reader’s current uncertainty.

A useful framework is:

Reader stageBetter anchor styleProblem awareness“How to choose the right…”Research phase“Compare the best…”Narrowing options“X vs Y comparison”Purchase readiness“See current deals on…”Product validation“Read the full review of…”

This structure is especially valuable for programmatic or template-driven affiliate sites because it allows internal links to adapt automatically based on page intent.

## Anchors should describe benefits, not just keywords

A common affiliate SEO mistake is writing anchors purely for keyword matching rather than click motivation.

For example:

* “best dehumidifier”
* “robot mower”
* “coffee grinder review”

These technically describe the destination, but they are weak because they lack specificity and user relevance.

Anchors become more persuasive when they reflect the reader’s actual goal.

Compare:

* “best office chair”
* “best office chairs for lower back pain”

Or:

* “VPN review”
* “VPNs that still work with Netflix UK”

The second versions imply a practical outcome.

Nielsen Norman Group recommends link labels that are specific, sincere, substantial, and succinct. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/better-link-labels/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nngroup.com">[nngroup.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nngroup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">better link labels</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">4Ss for Encouraging Clicks24 Mar 2019 — Write specific links that set sincere expectations and fulfill them, that are substantial enough...</span></span></span>

That “sincere” point matters for affiliate sites. Overpromising inside anchor text damages trust quickly.

Bad examples:

* “The perfect laptop for everyone”
* “The only hosting company you’ll ever need”
* “Guaranteed fastest VPN”

Better alternatives:

* “Hosting companies with strong beginner support”
* “Laptops with long battery life for travel”
* “VPNs tested for streaming performance”

These anchors sell the next step without sounding manipulative.

## Examples for guides, comparisons, reviews, and deals

Different money pages require different anchor styles. Repeating the same anchor formula everywhere creates predictable, low-performing internal links.

### Buying guide anchors

Buying guide anchors should reduce uncertainty and frame the decision.

Weak:

* “Buying guide”
* “Best products”
* “More options”

<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/better-link-labels/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nngroup.com">[Better:]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nngroup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">better link labels</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">4Ss for Encouraging Clicks24 Mar 2019 — Write specific links that set sincere expectations and fulfill them, that are substantial enough...</span></span></span>

* “How to choose the right pressure washer”
* “Best air purifiers for pet hair”
* “Beginner’s guide to mirrorless cameras”

These anchors work well from informational content because they continue the learning journey naturally.

## The risk of over-optimised anchor text

Commercial anchor text can become too aggressive when affiliate sites over-prioritise keyword repetition.

Problems appear when every link uses nearly identical wording:

* “best gaming laptop”
* “best gaming laptop reviews”
* “cheap gaming laptop deals”
* “top gaming laptop”

This creates three issues:

* The links feel repetitive to readers.
* The site looks mechanically optimised.
* Internal pathways become less informative.

Google’s guidance emphasises natural, descriptive anchors rather than manipulative repetition. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[Google for Developers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search CentralGood anchor text is descriptive, reasonably concise, and r...</span></span></span>

A stronger approach is semantic variation tied to genuine user intent:

* “Gaming laptops with strong cooling”
* “Best RTX laptops for students”
* “Portable gaming laptops under 15 inches”
* “Compare mid-range gaming laptops”

The destination page may still target the same commercial cluster, but the anchors reflect different entry motivations.

For large affiliate systems, this creates a scalable pattern:

* One money page
* Multiple intent-specific anchor variants
* Multiple supporting-page contexts

That improves both usability and conversion relevance.

## Anchor text should feel like navigation, not advertising

Readers click internal links more willingly when the anchor feels helpful rather than promotional.

This distinction is subtle but commercially important.

Compare these two examples inside an article about sleep quality:

* “Buy the top-rated weighted blanket”
* “Compare weighted blankets for hot sleepers”

The first feels like a sales push. The second feels like a useful continuation of research.

Affiliate sites that maintain this balance often generate better long-term revenue because readers continue exploring instead of resisting commercial intent.

This becomes especially important on informational pages where trust is still being established.

A practical rule is:

* Informational pages should usually link towards evaluation.
* Evaluation pages can link towards purchase.
* Purchase-ready pages can link towards retailers.

## Scaling anchor text across large affiliate sites

On large websites, anchor text cannot rely entirely on manual writing decisions. Systems need repeatable patterns that still feel specific.

A scalable approach is to build anchors from reusable components:

* Product category
* User problem
* Commercial format
* Audience qualifier
* Price range
* Use case

This allows semi-automated generation of useful anchors such as:

* “Best budget hiking boots for winter”
* “Compare cordless drills under £100”
* “Standing desks for small home offices”
* “Noise-cancelling headphones for flights”

The key is that the anchor still communicates a meaningful destination rather than becoming templated filler.

One useful quality check is simple:

Could a reader predict the destination page accurately from the anchor alone?

If not, the anchor is probably too vague.
