## Introduction

Choosing affiliate offers is not the same as choosing the highest commission rate. A 25% commission on a merchant that rarely converts, rejects many orders, has poor stock, or sends visitors to a weak landing page can earn less than a 3% commission from a trusted retailer with strong checkout flow and reliable tracking. For an affiliate website, the practical question is not “what percentage do they pay?” but “how much approved revenue does this offer produce per qualified visitor?” Networks increasingly expose this wider picture through metrics such as conversion rate, approval percentage, validation period and earnings per click, because these are closer to real publisher income than headline rate alone. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://help.awin.com/docs/awin-index-score" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: help.awin.com">[help.awin.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">help.awin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Index score</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Index score</span></span></span>

For a website built around affiliate links, offer selection affects topic choice, page structure, [comparison tables]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-best-of-affiliate-pa-55feea-comparison-tables-cl-5eb30a/' | relative_url }}), call-to-action wording, [internal linking]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-internal-l-d8b176/' | relative_url }}) and revenue per visitor. The best offer for a page is usually the one that fits the reader’s intent, inspires enough trust for the next click, and has programme rules that allow the affiliate to be paid fairly for the sale or lead they helped create.

## Commission Rates Can Mislead

Headline commission rates are attractive because they are simple. A programme paying 20% looks better than one paying 4%, and a fixed £80 lead payment looks better than a small retail percentage. The problem is that commission rate is only one part of the revenue path. Affiliate income depends on the combination of click-through rate, merchant conversion rate, average order value, commission rate, approval rate and tracking reliability. Awin’s own programme-quality framework reflects this by using four measures: conversion rate, approval percentage, validation period and earnings per click, rather than commission rate alone. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://help.awin.com/advertisers/docs/en/validating-transactions" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: help.awin.com">[help.awin.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">help.awin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Validating transactions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Validating transactions</span></span></span>

A simple comparison shows the problem. Offer A pays 20% on a £40 order, converts 0.5% of clicks, and approves 70% of tracked sales. Its expected approved revenue per 1,000 clicks is low. Offer B pays 5% on a £200 order, converts 4% of clicks, and approves 95% of sales. The lower-rate offer can produce far more money because more visitors buy, the basket is larger, and fewer commissions disappear after validation.

Earnings per click, or EPC, is useful because it compresses several moving parts into one performance number. Skimlinks explains EPC as commission paid divided by clicks, and says it is one of the key metrics publishers use to judge which [merchants]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-best-of-affiliate-pa-55feea-multiple-merchant-li-625ce1/' | relative_url }}) are likely to produce revenue. Its publisher merchant pages also show average commission rate, EPC, average conversion rate and average basket size, allowing publishers to compare their own results with wider network averages. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://advertisers.skimlinks.com/hc/en-us/articles/5068863952413-Why-is-my-recommended-rate-so-high" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: advertisers.skimlinks.com">[advertisers.skimlinks.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">advertisers.skimlinks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Why is my recommended rate so high? – Skimlinks Advertisers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Why is my recommended rate so high? – Skimlinks Advertisers</span></span></span>

That does not mean EPC should be followed blindly. Network-wide EPC may be influenced by cashback sites, coupon partners, paid media, email publishers, or large editorial partners with very different traffic from a content website. It is best treated as a starting signal: useful for shortlisting offers, but then tested against the site’s own pages, audience and intent.

## The Real Tradeoff Is Commission Versus Conversion

A strong affiliate offer usually wins in one of two ways. Either it has a high basket value with a modest commission, or it has a high commission with a strong conversion rate. The weakest offers are often those with “average everything”: an average basket, average commission, average trust, average landing page and average approval rate. They may look acceptable in a programme directory, but they rarely become the top earner on a serious affiliate page.

For practical page building, offer selection should start with the user’s stage of decision. A visitor searching “best cordless vacuum for pet hair” may respond well to trusted retailers with good stock, recognisable delivery options and easy returns. A visitor searching “accounting software free trial” may care more about trial length, sign-up friction, pricing clarity and whether the landing page explains the plan difference. In both cases, the offer needs to match what the visitor is trying to do next, not simply the offer with the largest percentage payout.

A useful decision rule is to compare offers by expected approved value, not advertised rate:

**Expected approved value per click = merchant conversion rate × average order value × commission rate × approval rate**

This turns offer choice into a repeatable selection problem. A website-generation system can use this logic to decide which merchants deserve primary buttons, which deserve secondary placement, and which should only appear when they uniquely fit a reader need.

## Merchant Trust and Landing Pages Decide Whether Clicks Become Money

The affiliate page can persuade the user to click, but the merchant page must finish the job. That makes merchant trust part of offer selection. A visitor who lands on a slow page, confusing product listing, hidden delivery charge, weak returns policy, or unfamiliar checkout may abandon the purchase even if the affiliate content did its job.

Checkout [evidence]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-best-of-affiliate-pa-55feea-evidence-blocks-affi-b0d439/' | relative_url }}) shows why this matters. Baymard Institute’s long-running cart abandonment research puts the average documented online shopping cart abandonment rate at about 70%, and its 2025 abandonment reasons include high extra costs, slow delivery, lack of trust with card details, forced account creation, complicated checkout, poor returns policy and site errors. These are merchant-side problems, but they directly reduce affiliate earnings when visitors are sent there. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: baymard.com">[Baymard Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">baymard.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cart abandonment rate</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">cart abandonment rate</span></span></span>

For affiliate offer selection, this means the merchant page should be reviewed like a user would review it:

* **Does the landing page match the promise?** If the affiliate page recommends a specific product, the click should land on that product or a highly relevant category, not a generic homepage.
* **Is the product actually in stock?** Out-of-stock products waste high-intent clicks and damage trust in the affiliate site.
* **Are price, delivery and returns clear before checkout?** [Hidden costs]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-review-pag-f66da7-ownership-cost-check-846203/' | relative_url }}) and poor returns information are common reasons for abandonment.
* **Does the merchant look credible?** Recognisable payment methods, clear contact details, reviews, delivery information and secure checkout all affect whether visitors continue.
* **Does the mobile journey work?** Many affiliate clicks happen on mobile; a desktop-friendly merchant page may still leak revenue if mobile checkout is poor.

This is where a lower commission from a familiar retailer can outperform a higher commission from a weak merchant. The affiliate site is not just selling the product; it is choosing where to send user trust.

## Approval Rates and Validation Periods Are Part of the Offer

An affiliate commission is not truly earned when it appears as pending. It becomes reliable revenue only after the merchant validates the transaction. Awin describes validation as the process where a tracked purchase appears as pending and is then approved or declined based on issues such as returns, cancellations, payment failure, or whether the transaction meets programme terms. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://help.awin.com/advertisers/docs/en/auto-validation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: help.awin.com">[help.awin.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">help.awin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on awin.com.</span></span></span>

This is why approval percentage matters. Two programmes may pay the same rate, but one may approve nearly all transactions while another declines many because of returns, cancellations, voucher conflicts, lead-quality rules, or restricted product categories. A programme with a high reversal rate can look profitable in early reporting and disappoint later when commissions are rejected.

Validation period also affects cash flow and testing speed. Awin explains auto-validation as the period after which a transaction is automatically approved if the advertiser has not manually approved or declined it, with different maximum periods depending on platform plan or contract. A long validation period may be acceptable for travel, finance or high-return categories, but it slows feedback for a scalable content system trying to decide which offers and page patterns to expand. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://help.awin.com/advertisers/docs/en/understanding-cookies-in-affiliate-marketing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: help.awin.com">[help.awin.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">help.awin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">understanding cookies in affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">understanding cookies in affiliate marketing</span></span></span>

For offer selection, approval quality should influence placement. A merchant with strong conversion but slow or uncertain approval may still be worth testing, but it should not dominate a page until approved earnings confirm the early numbers.

## Tracking Rules Can Make or Break an Otherwise Good Offer

Affiliate marketing depends on tracked links and attribution rules. Amazon’s UK Associates agreement, for example, says associates monetise their site by placing special tagged links, and commission is paid for qualifying purchases subject to the programme’s commission statement and limitations. It also warns that links must use the proper tagged formats. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk/help/operating/agreement" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk">[affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Amazon.co.uk Associates Central</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Amazon.co.uk Associates Central</span></span></span>

The important lesson is that each programme has its own rules about what counts. Some programmes use short attribution windows, others use 30, 60 or 90 days. Some exclude certain products, self-referrals, voucher-code conflicts, app purchases, paid-search traffic, or purchases that are not correctly tracked. Amazon’s policy pages list programme policies and exclusions, while its wider operating agreement incorporates participation requirements, commission statements and other rules into the agreement. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk/help/operating/policies/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk">[affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Amazon.co.uk Associates Central</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Amazon.co.uk Associates Central</span></span></span>

Cookie duration is especially important when the product has a long decision cycle. A same-day impulse purchase can work with a short window. A sofa, business software subscription, insurance policy, holiday booking or high-ticket appliance may involve several visits and comparisons. In those categories, a longer attribution window can be more commercially valuable than a slightly higher rate.

Tracking reliability is also affected by the modern web. Browser privacy changes, cookie restrictions and cross-device journeys can all reduce attribution. For a public-facing affiliate site, the practical response is not to over-explain tracking technology, but to prefer programmes with clear network reporting, stable deep links, transparent terms, and enough data to compare clicks, conversions and approved commissions over time.

## Offer Selection Should Match Page Type

Different [page types]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-buyer-intent-revenue-fe10e4-affiliate-page-types-956d68/' | relative_url }}) need different offer logic. A scalable affiliate website should not use one merchant-selection rule across every page.

A **best products** page needs offers with strong product availability, recognisable buying routes and category coverage. The best merchant may be a retailer with broad stock and high buyer trust rather than the manufacturer programme with the highest rate.

A **single product review** page needs the most relevant live product destination. If the product is often out of stock, the page may need multiple retailer options so the reader can still buy without bouncing back to search results.

A **comparison page** needs merchants that allow fair side-by-side routing. If one offer pays more but has poor stock or limited delivery, it should not be forced into the primary position. The page earns trust by helping the reader choose, not by hiding the better buying route.

A **voucher or deals page** needs careful programme-rule checks. Some merchants restrict coupon use, some de-duplicate against other partners, and some decline transactions when unauthorised codes are used. That can make advertised commission less reliable than it appears.

A **lead-generation page** needs close attention to approval rules. In finance, insurance, education, B2B software and home services, a “lead” may only be payable if it meets quality [criteria]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-comparison-5755f1-category-specific-cr-a92f21/' | relative_url }}) such as location, credit profile, valid contact details or completed follow-up. The payout may be high, but approval risk is also higher.

## A Repeatable Offer Scoring Framework

For a high-volume affiliate website system, offer choice should be standardised enough to scale but flexible enough to respect user intent. A useful scoring model can group checks into four layers.

**1. Commercial value**

This is the expected value of the offer if the visitor clicks. It includes commission rate, average order value, conversion rate, EPC, approval percentage and validation period. When a network provides metrics such as EPC, average basket size and conversion rate, they should be used for initial shortlisting and then replaced or adjusted with the site’s own performance data once available. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://support.skimlinks.com/hc/en-us/articles/27523467096605-Performance-Metrics-on-the-Merchant-Profile-Page" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: support.skimlinks.com">[support.skimlinks.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">support.skimlinks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Performance Metrics on the Merchant Profile Page – Skimlinks Publishers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Performance Metrics on the Merchant Profile Page – Skimlinks Publishers</span></span></span>

**2. Reader fit**

This asks whether the offer fits the page intent. A high-commission luxury merchant may be wrong for a “best budget” page. A retailer with limited UK delivery may be wrong for a UK-focused site. A merchant with only one brand may be wrong for a comparison page unless the reader is already brand-aware.

**3. Merchant experience**

This includes stock, price competitiveness, landing-page relevance, checkout clarity, delivery options, returns policy, trust signals and mobile usability. Baymard’s abandonment findings show that issues such as extra costs, slow delivery, lack of trust and complicated checkout directly affect whether users complete purchases. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: baymard.com">[Baymard Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">baymard.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cart abandonment rate</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">cart abandonment rate</span></span></span>

**4. Programme reliability**

This includes attribution window, tracking method, deep-link stability, product exclusions, self-referral rules, validation process, payment timing, and whether the programme is active and well-managed. Skimlinks merchant profile data includes estimated payment time and estimated attribution window, which are useful examples of the kind of non-rate information publishers need when comparing merchants. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://support.skimlinks.com/hc/en-us/articles/25941239595549-Using-the-Skimlinks-Editor-Toolbar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: support.skimlinks.com">[support.skimlinks.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">support.skimlinks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">25941239595549 Using the Skimlinks Editor Toolbar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">25941239595549 Using the Skimlinks Editor Toolbar</span></span></span>

A practical scoring outcome might classify offers as:

* **Primary offer:** strong [intent match]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-offer-sele-904ed4-offer-intent-match-6047d8/' | relative_url }}), good merchant trust, acceptable commission, reliable tracking and approval.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* **Secondary offer:** useful alternative for stock, price, delivery, brand preference or reader choice.
* **Test offer:** promising rate or EPC, but not enough site-specific data yet.
* **Avoid or demote:** high rate but poor conversion path, weak trust, restrictive terms, low approval, or poor stock.

</div>

## Why Trust Can Beat Payout

Trust affects both conversion and long-term site value. If a visitor clicks from a comparison page to a merchant that feels unreliable, the immediate sale may be lost. More importantly, the affiliate site’s recommendation quality is damaged. The reader may return to search results, ignore future calls to action, or distrust other recommendations on the site.

Major publishers often separate affiliate monetisation from editorial selection to protect credibility. WIRED’s UK affiliate policy says its affiliate schemes do not affect which products editors cover or recommend, and that it lists a range of retailers, with some links not connected to affiliate programmes. That is a publisher-specific policy, not a universal affiliate rule, but it illustrates the commercial logic: reader trust is an asset, not a decoration. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.wired.com/story/affiliate-link-policy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">s editorial policy on affiliate links</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">s editorial policy on affiliate links</span></span></span>

For smaller affiliate sites, the same principle can be applied operationally. Do not automatically rank merchants by payout. Rank them by usefulness to the reader and expected approved value. When the best reader option is non-affiliate or lower-paying, including it can make the page more credible and improve the performance of the affiliate offers that remain.

## Disclosure Is Also Part of Offer Quality

Offer selection is not only a revenue decision; it is also a compliance and trust decision. The UK ASA describes affiliate marketing as performance-based advertising and says affiliates are effectively secondary advertisers because they earn money in direct proportion to the interest they generate. The CAP Code requires marketing communications to be obviously identifiable as such, and ASA guidance explains that affiliate content should be labelled clearly where the commercial nature is not already obvious. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ASAOnline Affiliate Marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAOnline Affiliate Marketing</span></span></span>

This matters for offer selection because some page templates carry more disclosure risk than others. A product round-up made entirely of affiliate-linked products may need clearer up-front labelling than a general informational article containing a few affiliate links. ASA guidance also warns that vague or hidden disclosures may be insufficient, especially where only some links are commercial. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/news/get-yourself-affiliated-with-the-rules-on-affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ASAGet yourself affiliated with the rules on affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAGet yourself affiliated with the rules on affiliate marketing</span></span></span>

A good affiliate website should therefore choose offers it is comfortable disclosing. If the page would feel misleading once the affiliate relationship is made clear, the offer probably does not fit the content. Transparent disclosure does not have to kill conversions; it can support trust when the recommendation is genuinely useful and the page makes clear why the merchant is being suggested.

## Testing Offers Without Rebuilding Every Page

Because affiliate performance varies by audience and intent, offer selection should be tested rather than assumed. The scalable approach is not to manually rewrite every page, but to standardise offer slots and measure how each merchant performs within them.

Useful tests include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* **Primary button test:** compare which merchant earns more approved revenue when placed as the main call to action.
* **Multi-retailer test:** offer two or three merchants where stock, delivery or price varies, then measure total approved earnings and click distribution.
* **Landing-page test:** send clicks to product pages, category pages or offer-specific landing pages depending on intent.
* **Offer-order test:** compare ranking by commission, EPC, reader fit or price competitiveness.
* **Fallback test:** monitor whether secondary merchants recover revenue when the primary merchant is out of stock.

</div>

The key measurement is not just click-through rate. A merchant can attract many clicks because the button is prominent or the brand is familiar, but still produce poor approved revenue. The decision metric should be approved earnings per page visitor, supported by click-through rate, merchant conversion rate, approval rate and refund or reversal patterns.

## What This Means for Website Systems

For a software-driven affiliate website system, offer selection should be treated as a decision cluster rather than a manual afterthought. The system needs to know which pages are commercial, what intent they serve, which merchants fit that intent, and how reliable each offer is after the click.

At the template level, this affects how pages are built. A “best” page may need a merchant module that supports multiple retailer buttons, stock notes and price-positioning language. A review page may need a primary offer plus fallback retailers. A comparison page may need merchant-neutral product scoring before affiliate routing. A deal page may need stricter checks for expiry, voucher rules and validation risk.

At the cluster level, successful offer patterns can be reused. If one retailer consistently converts well for “small garden storage” pages, it may deserve wider testing across adjacent garden categories. If a high-commission merchant underperforms across several pages because of poor stock or low approval, it should be demoted across the cluster rather than repeatedly tested from scratch.

The central rule is simple: choose offers by expected approved value and reader fit, not by headline commission. The best affiliate offer is the one that helps the visitor complete the next useful action and reliably pays when that action creates value.
