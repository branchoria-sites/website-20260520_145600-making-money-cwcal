## Introduction

Internal linking is the system that moves readers from broad advice into the pages where [affiliate revenue]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-conversion-fac650-affiliate-revenue-le-8c2b2f/' | relative_url }}) is most likely to happen: comparisons, buying guides, product reviews, category hubs, deal pages, and merchant-facing recommendation pages. On an affiliate website, links are not just decorative SEO extras. They are the route between a reader’s question and a commercial decision.


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_affiliate_internal_l_d8b176-overview.png" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Internal Linking" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
A strong internal linking system does two jobs at once. It helps search engines discover important pages and understand how topics relate, and it helps readers move from “I need to understand this” to “I am ready to compare or buy”. Google says links help it find pages and that [anchor text]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-internal-l-d8b176-commercial-anchor-te-cda81b/' | relative_url }}) helps both users and Google understand the linked page. It also says ecommerce navigation and cross-page links can affect how Google understands a site’s structure and the relative importance of pages. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[Google for Developers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search Central | Documentation | Google for DevelopersFebruary 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2025</span></span></span>

For affiliate sites, the practical goal is simple: each supporting page should lead to the most useful next commercial page at the moment the reader is ready for it.

## Supporting pages versus money pages

An affiliate site usually contains two different kinds of page, even when both look like ordinary articles to the reader. Supporting pages attract and educate visitors. Money pages help those visitors compare, choose, click, and buy.

A supporting page might answer “what size dehumidifier do I need?”, “are robot lawn mowers safe for pets?”, or “how much storage space do garden cushions need?”. These pages can attract search traffic and build trust, but they are often too early in the journey for a direct merchant click to be the most useful next step. The reader is still diagnosing the problem.

A money page sits closer to the transaction. It might be “best dehumidifiers for bedrooms”, “best robot lawn mowers for small gardens”, “Keter vs Lifetime garden storage boxes”, or a detailed review of a specific model. These pages can carry [comparison tables]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-best-of-affiliate-pa-55feea-comparison-tables-cl-5eb30a/' | relative_url }}), ranked recommendations, product filters, retailer links, offer blocks, and clearer calls to action because the reader’s intent is more commercial.

The internal link between the two [page types]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-buyer-intent-revenue-fe10e4-affiliate-page-types-956d68/' | relative_url }}) is the bridge. A reader who has just learnt that tank size, extraction rate, and room temperature affect dehumidifier choice is far more likely to click a link to “compare the best dehumidifiers for cold bedrooms” than a vague “read more”. This fits Google’s guidance that link text should be descriptive, concise, and relevant to the page it links to. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[Google for Developers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search Central | Documentation | Google for DevelopersFebruary 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2025</span></span></span>

The distinction matters because affiliate revenue is rarely created by traffic alone. A supporting page may bring in readers, but the money page usually does the commercial work. The internal linking system decides whether those readers ever reach it.

A useful classification is:

* **Supporting pages** explain, define, diagnose, compare concepts, answer questions, or narrow the reader’s problem.
* **Money pages** compare products, recommend options, review specific items, list suitable [merchants]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-best-of-affiliate-pa-55feea-multiple-merchant-li-625ce1/' | relative_url }}), or present buying routes.
* **Internal links** move the reader from understanding the problem to evaluating the solution.

A common affiliate-site failure is to publish a large library of informational articles without enough intentional paths into the pages that earn. The opposite failure is to force every page towards the same high-commission destination, even when the reader’s intent does not fit. Both mistakes waste traffic. The first hides commercial pages; the second damages trust and relevance.

## Topic clusters should act like conversion paths

Topic clusters are often described as an SEO structure: a central hub page supported by related articles. For affiliate sites, that definition is too limited. A topic cluster should also behave like a conversion path.

Take a cluster about home coffee machines. A broad guide might explain pod machines, bean-to-cup machines, espresso machines, running costs, cleaning, counter space, milk frothing, and warranty issues. The supporting pages might answer narrow questions such as “are bean-to-cup machines expensive to run?” or “what is a 15-bar coffee machine?”. The money pages might compare “best bean-to-cup coffee machines”, “best coffee machines under £500”, “Sage vs De’Longhi coffee machines”, and individual product reviews.

The cluster is not just a group of related pages. It is a set of routes for readers at different stages of the buying journey. Someone reading about running costs may need a comparison of efficient machines. Someone comparing Sage and De’Longhi may need product reviews. Someone reading a review may need a broader “best for small kitchens” guide if that product is not right for them.

Google’s ecommerce site-structure guidance is useful here even though affiliate sites do not usually own the products they recommend. Google says it analyses relationships between pages based on linkages, and that menus and cross-page links can influence its understanding of site structure. It also gives a clear hierarchy example: menus link to categories, categories to subcategories, and subcategories to products. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[Google for Developers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search Central | Documentation | Google for DevelopersFebruary 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2025</span></span></span>

Affiliate sites can adapt that pattern:

1. **Broad topic hub:** “Coffee machines for home”
2. **Supporting guides:** “Pod vs bean-to-cup”, “coffee machine running costs”, “milk frother types”
3. **[Comparison pages]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-comparison-5755f1/' | relative_url }}):** “Sage vs De’Longhi”, “bean-to-cup vs pod machines”
4. **Money pages:** “Best bean-to-cup coffee machines”, “best coffee machines for small kitchens”
5. **Product reviews:** reviews of specific models, each linking back to broader comparisons and alternatives

The direction of movement matters. A reader should not be trapped in an endless loop of informational articles. The cluster should create natural opportunities to advance towards comparison and purchase-focused pages.

Ecommerce research supports this navigation-first view. Baymard describes category pages as navigational hubs that organise a product catalogue and help shoppers find the right subcategory or product type. Its research also notes that users may abandon sites when they cannot find where to look, and that category pages can surface buying guides and other decision-support content when users need guidance. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://baymard.com/learn/ecommerce-category-page" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: baymard.com">[Baymard Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">baymard.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Institute Ecommerce Category Pages: Definition, Usage, &amp; Design Tips – Baymard</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Baymard InstituteEcommerce Category Pages: Definition, Usage, &amp; Design Tips – Baymard...</span></span></span>

For affiliate sites, money pages often play a similar hub role. They are not merely pages with affiliate buttons. The best ones help readers choose a path: by budget, use case, brand, feature, problem, or level of commitment.

## Link placement should match intent

An internal link works best when it appears at the point where the reader naturally wants the next step. This is different from adding a link every time a keyword appears.

On an early-stage informational page, the reader may not yet be ready for product recommendations. If a page answers “why is my bedroom damp?”, the first job is to explain condensation, ventilation, humidity, heating, leaks, and mould risk. A link to a dehumidifier buying guide becomes more useful after the page has explained when a dehumidifier is actually relevant.

On a comparison page, commercial links can appear earlier. A reader searching for “dehumidifier vs air purifier” or “cordless drill vs impact driver” is already evaluating options. The page can link sooner to buying guides, product comparisons, or reviews because the reader’s intent is closer to action.

Research on information scent helps explain why this matters. Information scent is the set of cues that tells users whether a link is likely to lead to what they want. A 2026 paper on trial-and-error navigation found that users often make premature selections, choose wrong links, and backtrack when navigation cues are ambiguous or deeply nested. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11759" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXivModeling Trial-and-Error Navigation With a Sequential Decision Model of Information ScentMarch 12, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 12, 2026</span></span></span>

For affiliate sites, weak information scent is a revenue problem. If the reader cannot tell what a link will do, they may ignore it. If the link sends them somewhere that does not match their need, they may leave. Clear link labels and well-timed placement are therefore part of conversion design, not just site tidiness.

A practical placement rule is:

* **Before the reader understands the problem:** link to definitions, guides, and diagnostic pages.
* **After the reader understands the [criteria]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-affiliate-comparison-5755f1-category-specific-cr-a92f21/' | relative_url }}):** link to buying guides and “best for” pages.
* **When the reader is comparing options:** link to comparisons, reviews, and alternative lists.
* **When the reader is ready to act:** link to merchants, deals, product [tables]({{ 'making-money-from-creating-websites-containing-aff/making-money-from-cr-f649af-best-of-affiliate-pa-55feea-comparison-tables-cl-5eb30a/' | relative_url }}), and calls to action.

For example, on a page about “how to choose a pressure washer for patios”, the first internal links might support understanding: patio surface types, water pressure, detergent use, storage, and safety. Later, after the page explains the buying criteria, links to “best pressure washers for patios” or “compact pressure washers for small gardens” feel much more helpful.

## Anchor text should describe the commercial next step

Anchor text is the clickable wording of a link. In an affiliate linking system, it should do more than contain a keyword. It should set a clear expectation for the next page.

Google’s link guidance says anchor text helps users and Google make sense of content, and that good anchor text is descriptive, reasonably concise, and relevant to both the current page and the linked page. It also warns that Google can only reliably crawl links when they use proper anchor elements with an `href` attribute. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[Google for Developers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search Central | Documentation | Google for DevelopersFebruary 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2025</span></span></span>

For an affiliate site, this turns into a simple public-facing rule: **the reader should know what kind of commercial page they are about to visit before they click**.

Weak anchors include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* “click here”
* “this guide”
* “our top picks”
* “read more”
* “best option”

</div>

Better anchors are more specific:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* “compare the best dehumidifiers for bedrooms”
* “see cordless drills for light DIY jobs”
* “read our Sage Barista Express review”
* “compare garden storage boxes for small patios”
* “check robot lawn mowers for uneven gardens”

</div>

This does not mean every anchor should be an exact-match keyword. In fact, a scalable system should allow natural variation so the link fits the sentence. A guide on running costs might link with “energy-efficient dehumidifiers”, while a guide on room size might link with “dehumidifiers for small bedrooms”. Both can point to the same money page if the destination genuinely covers both angles.

The important test is not whether the anchor is mechanically optimised. It is whether the anchor answers the reader’s implied question: “What will I get if I click this?”

## Money pages need the right incoming links

A money page can be commercially strong and still underperform if the rest of the site barely points to it. Internal links tell both readers and search engines which pages matter, but relevance is more important than raw link count.

Google’s ecommerce guidance says it can use signals such as the number of links needed to reach a page and the number of links to a page to infer relative importance. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[Google for Developers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersSEO Link Best Practices for Google | Google Search Central | Documentation | Google for DevelopersFebruary 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2025</span></span></span> For an affiliate website, that means a commercially important page should not sit four or five clicks deep with only one old article linking to it.

The source pages should be chosen by intent. A page on “how to stop condensation on windows” can naturally point to “best dehumidifiers for bedrooms”. A page on “pet hair vacuum features explained” can point to “best vacuum cleaners for pet hair”. A page on “are refurbished phones safe?” can point to “best refurbished phone retailers” or “refurbished iPhone buying guide”. These routes work because the supporting page creates the need for the money page.

The wrong approach is to link every supporting article to the same high-value page regardless of context. That might increase the internal link count, but it does not necessarily improve the reader journey. A garden furniture maintenance article does not automatically need to link to every garden furniture buying guide. It should link to the one or two destinations that match the reader’s next decision.

A scalable rule set can help:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* **One primary commercial destination per supporting page:** choose the money page that best matches the reader’s next intent.
* **One or two secondary routes:** add related guides or comparisons for readers who are not yet ready for the main money page.
* **Contextual links before generic blocks:** links inside relevant paragraphs usually explain the next step better than a standard “related articles” list alone.
* **Money pages link back to broader choices:** a product review should link to alternatives and category comparisons, not only to a merchant.

</div>

This last point is often missed. A reader who decides not to buy the reviewed product may still be valuable. If the review links only to a merchant, the journey ends. If it also links to “best alternatives”, “compare similar models”, or “best budget options”, the site keeps helping the reader instead of losing them.

## Internal linking has shifted from PageRank sculpting to reader routing

Internal linking has a long SEO history. Older discussions often focused on PageRank, link equity, crawl depth, and whether internal links could be used to push ranking power to selected pages. Those ideas still matter, because search engines use links to discover and interpret pages. But for affiliate sites, the more useful modern frame is reader routing.

The historical shift is that links are no longer just treated as votes or crawl paths. They are also product-discovery paths, trust signals, and conversion routes. Wired’s account of the hyperlink’s commercial evolution notes how Google’s PageRank made links central to ranking incentives and changed how publishers and businesses used them. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.wired.com/story/google-link-hyperlink-seo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How Google warped the hyperlink</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How Google warped the hyperlink</span></span></span> The lesson for affiliate sites is not to abandon SEO thinking, but to avoid reducing every link to a ranking tactic.

A money-page routing system asks different questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* What did the reader come here to understand?
* What commercial question will they be ready to ask next?
* Which money page answers that next question best?
* Does the anchor text make that next step obvious?
* Does the destination page continue the journey rather than forcing a premature sale?

</div>

This is a more useful frame for high-volume affiliate publishing because it can be standardised without becoming mechanical. A software-driven content system can classify page roles, identify intent relationships, and propose likely commercial destinations. But the underlying logic should remain reader-facing: each link should help the visitor make progress.

## Disclosure should travel with the commercial journey

Internal links that move readers towards affiliate pages should not disguise the commercial nature of those pages. Trust is part of the conversion path.

In the UK, CAP guidance describes affiliate marketing as performance-based marketing where an affiliate is rewarded for customers attracted through click-throughs or sales. It also says affiliates are effectively secondary advertisers because they earn in direct proportion to the interest they generate, and that affiliate marketing must be obviously identifiable. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ASAOnline Affiliate Marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAOnline Affiliate Marketing</span></span></span> CAP’s 2024 guidance also warns that abbreviations such as “Aff” may not be clear enough to consumers when used alone. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.asa.org.uk/news/get-yourself-affiliated-with-the-rules-on-affiliate-marketing.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asa.org.uk">[ASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ASAGet yourself affiliated with the rules on affiliate marketing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASAGet yourself affiliated with the rules on affiliate marketing</span></span></span>

A supporting page can link to a money page, but the destination page should make the affiliate relationship clear before or when the reader encounters affiliate links. That does not mean every internal link from an educational article needs a heavy warning label. It means the destination should be transparent, and the reader should not be surprised to find affiliate recommendations after clicking a buying-guide-style link.

## Scalable linking patterns for affiliate sites

A scalable affiliate site needs repeatable linking patterns, not one-off editorial improvisation on every page. The goal is to standardise good judgement.

A useful system starts by assigning each page a role. That role determines which internal links are expected.

**Question pages** answer a narrow query and usually link to one broader guide plus one money page. For example, “what size garden storage box do I need?” might link to a storage size guide and to “best garden storage boxes for small patios”.

**Explainer pages** teach the criteria that affect a purchase. They should link to comparisons and “best for” money pages once those criteria have been explained.

**Comparison pages** sit closer to purchase intent. They should link to relevant product reviews, buying guides, merchant-facing recommendation pages, and alternative comparisons.

**Product reviews** should link to the merchant, but also to broader category pages and alternatives. This catches readers who are interested in the category but not sold on that one product.

**Category hubs** should organise the cluster. They should link to subcategories, buying guides, comparisons, and high-value reviews. Baymard’s research on ecommerce category pages supports this hub function: category pages help users orient themselves, navigate deeper, and find the right product type rather than being dropped into an overwhelming product list. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://baymard.com/learn/ecommerce-category-page" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: baymard.com">[Baymard Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">baymard.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Institute Ecommerce Category Pages: Definition, Usage, &amp; Design Tips – Baymard</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Baymard InstituteEcommerce Category Pages: Definition, Usage, &amp; Design Tips – Baymard...</span></span></span>

For high-volume production, the most useful linking fields are not merely “related posts”. They are intent-specific destinations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* primary money page
* supporting guide
* comparison page
* product review
* alternative or fallback page
* parent hub
* sibling topic
* merchant or offer page, where appropriate

</div>

This makes the system more resilient. If a product goes out of stock, a commission rate changes, or a merchant becomes less attractive, the site can still route readers to a broader comparison or alternative page instead of losing the commercial path entirely.

## Common failure modes

The most expensive internal linking mistakes on affiliate sites are usually not obscure technical issues. They are journey-design problems.

**Orphaned money pages** are pages with little or no internal support. They may contain strong comparisons and affiliate offers, but readers rarely reach them from the broader site. Search engines may also receive weaker signals that these pages are important.

**Informational loops** happen when articles link mainly to other articles. This can increase page views but fail to move readers towards comparison or purchase pages. A reader learns more and more, but never reaches the page designed to help them choose.

**Premature commercial pushing** happens when a page links to product recommendations before answering the reader’s actual question. This can feel like a hard sell and weaken trust.

**Generic anchor text** weakens the next step. “Read more” does not tell a reader whether they are going to a review, comparison, buying guide, or merchant offer. It also gives search engines less context than a descriptive anchor.

**One-size-fits-all money links** send too many pages to the same commercial destination. This can happen when a system automatically links every mention of “coffee machine” to one “best coffee machines” page, even when the reader actually needs a cleaning guide, a running-cost comparison, or a specific model review.

**Commercial dead ends** occur when a product review links only to a merchant. If the reader does not want that item, there is no next step. Better reviews link to alternatives, broader category pages, and relevant comparisons.

The fix is to audit internal links by user flow rather than by link count alone. For each supporting page, ask what commercial question the reader is now ready to answer. For each money page, ask which supporting pages should naturally send readers there.

## A practical model for moving readers towards money pages

The most reliable affiliate internal linking model has three stages: clarify, qualify, and route.

**Clarify the problem.** The supporting page answers the reader’s immediate question. It should not rush to a product link before the reader understands the issue. A page about damp bedrooms should explain causes and practical checks before recommending dehumidifiers.

**Qualify the intent.** The page then introduces the buying criteria that matter: room size, budget, safety, compatibility, warranty, merchant reliability, running cost, product type, or use case. This is where the reader becomes more commercially qualified.

**Route to the right money page.** The internal link points to the destination that matches the reader’s clarified need. For one reader, that may be a comparison page. For another, it may be a “best for small flats” guide. For another, it may be a specific review or merchant offer.

A pressure washer example shows the difference. A weak page about patio cleaning might end with a generic link to “pressure washer deals”. A stronger page explains patio material, water pressure, detergent, storage, hose length, and risk of damage. It then links to “best pressure washers for patios” or “compact pressure washers for small gardens”. The reader reaches the money page with clearer intent, and the money page has a better chance of earning because the next click now makes sense.

That is the core value of internal linking for affiliate sites. It turns a collection of pages into a revenue path: broad advice leads to clearer criteria, clearer criteria lead to comparison, and comparison leads to a commercial click when the reader is ready.
